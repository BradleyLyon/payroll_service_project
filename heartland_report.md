# Heartland — Company Brief & Pitch-Angle Playbook
*For the Simon lead-gen project · July 2026*

---

## 1. Who Heartland is

- Founded 1997 (Princeton, NJ) by Robert Carr. Grew into one of the largest US payment processors.
- Acquired by **Global Payments (NYSE: GPN)** in 2016 for ~$4.3B. Important: **Heartland accounts are officially transitioning to the Global Payments brand through 2026**, and their POS products are being unified under a single brand called **"Genius"** (e.g. "Genius for Restaurants"). Expect leads to hear both names.
- Serves roughly ~1,000,000 businesses; heavy focus on small/mid-size merchants, with restaurants as a core vertical.
- Historical note: suffered a massive 2008 card data breach; responded by building end-to-end encryption ("E3") and now markets heavily on security + a breach warranty. Known for US-based support with fast answer times.

## 2. Main services (what Simon can sell)

**Payments (bread and butter)**
- Credit/debit card processing, in-store, online, mobile. EMV, contactless, mobile wallets.
- E3 end-to-end encryption, PCI-compliant hardware, breach warranty.
- Surcharging / cash-discount programs, instant deposits, billing & invoicing.
- Gift card + loyalty programs.

**Point of Sale (Genius / Heartland Restaurant)**
- Cloud-based POS for restaurants (counter service through fine dining) and retail. Terminals, kiosks, KDS, handhelds.
- Online ordering + delivery integration, table management, tip payout tools.
- Inventory, real-time reporting, built-in CRM, 100+ third-party integrations, open API.
- Built-in employee tools: time & attendance, breaks, scheduling, tip payouts.

**Payroll+ / HR**
- Full payroll processing: tax filing, direct deposit, W-2s/1099s, deductions, garnishments, new-hire reporting.
- Hiring: applicant tracking system, job postings, onboarding paperwork (digital W-4/I-9), **WOTC (Work Opportunity Tax Credit) screening**.
- ACA reporting (large-employer status, 1094-C/1095-C), benefits integration (Ease), labor-law posters, HR coaching add-on.
- Tiered plans (Essentials / Advanced / Complete) — more HR = higher tier.

**Other**
- Business lending/capital via partners (24–48hr terms).
- eCommerce, gift/loyalty, school & campus payments (not relevant to us).

**Competitors:** Toast, Square, Clover/Fiserv, Stripe, ADP/Paychex/Gusto (payroll side).

## 3. Known weaknesses (objections you WILL hear)

- Reputation for **add-on fees**: PCI non-compliance fee (~$125/mo), annual reporting fee, non-EMV fees, supplies fees, etc. Rates generally higher than budget processors.
- BBB rating B-, mixed reviews; common complaints = billing and cancellation friction. $295 early termination fee per location.
- Payroll product is solid but basic vs. Gusto/ADP; strongest when **bundled** with Heartland POS/payments.
- Takeaway for pitching: **do not lead with "we'll save you money on rates"** — that's the pitch every restaurant owner ignores, and it's not Heartland's strong suit. Lead with bundling, compliance, and support.

## 4. Niche problem angles (the core of this report)

The plan: find businesses that are *violating something, missing a credit, or duct-taping a workflow* that a Heartland product fixes. Approach with the specific problem, not a generic sales call.

### A. NY tip & wage compliance (strongest restaurant angle)
NY tip law is a minefield and enforcement/lawsuits are common:
- **Tip credit**: employers must give written tip-credit notice (in the employee's language, signed) or they lose the credit — $50/day penalties up to $5k/yr per employee. Must track daily that wages+tips ≥ minimum wage.
- **80/20 rule**: tipped staff spending >20% of time on non-tipped work invalidates the tip credit.
- **Tip pooling**: managers can never participate; records must be kept ~6 years; contribution formulas must be documented.
- **Service charges**: mandatory service charges count as gratuities unless clearly disclosed otherwise — mislabeling triggers lawsuits.
- **Wage statements**: missing/inaccurate pay stubs = $250/day up to $5k/yr per employee.
- Real settlements are large (e.g. a Staten Island restaurant paid $700k over wage theft claims).

**Pitch:** small independents running tips on paper logs or spreadsheets are one disgruntled employee away from a lawsuit. Heartland POS (tip tracking/payout) + Payroll (records, wage statements) is the compliance fix. New openings especially — they haven't set anything up yet.

### B. New federal tip reporting (2026 hook — timely)
The "no tax on tips" provision (OBBBA) means employees can deduct up to $25k of qualified tips — but **employers must report tips with new W-2 codes**. Restaurants on manual payroll or generic providers may botch this and cost their staff the deduction. Fresh, specific, and nobody else is cold-calling about it yet.

### C. Money left on the table (undercutting themselves)
- **WOTC**: restaurants constantly hire from WOTC-eligible groups and never claim the federal credit because screening is annoying. Heartland's ATS screens automatically. "You're forfeiting tax credits every time you hire" is a strong opener.
- **ACA**: growing businesses crossing ~50 FTEs without tracking large-employer status face 1094-C/1095-C reporting they don't know about.
- **Surcharge compliance (NY)**: NY law requires the surcharged total price to be posted, not just "3% fee added." Places doing sloppy cash-discount/surcharge signage are exposed — and Heartland runs compliant surcharge programs.
- **NY pay frequency**: manual workers (most restaurant staff) must generally be paid **weekly** in NY — biweekly payroll is a live class-action trend. A payroll provider that sets this up right is the fix.

### D. Operational duct tape (visible from outside — feeds the bot)
- No website / no DoorDash (existing Simon angle — he can bundle both). Pre-opens from the SLA pipeline.
- In-house-only or phone-only ordering; third-party-only delivery with no direct ordering (losing 15–30% to commissions → "own your ordering" pitch).
- GoDaddy-hosted site → easy hosting/site cross-sell (existing angle).
- On competitor platform (Toast/Square) approaching known pain points: Toast's per-order consumer fees, Square's rate hikes — conversion batch material.
- Cash-only or old countertop terminal visible in Google/Yelp photos → whole-stack modernization lead, and likely non-EMV/PCI exposure.

### E. Beyond restaurants (from Simon's notes)
- Construction: **certified payroll** for public works — a genuine sore point Heartland payroll can address; building permits are the lead source.
- Nonprofits: identifiable from public data, need low-cost processing + payroll.
- New leadership / ownership changes: contracts are up for review.

## 5. What gets ignored (oversaturation notes)

Restaurant owners are the most cold-called merchant category in existence. Pitches that get hung up on:
- "We can lower your credit card processing rates" (everyone says it; owners assume hidden fees — and with Heartland it's a weak lead anyway)
- Generic "POS demo" calls with no knowledge of the business
- Anything during service hours (call 2:30–4:30pm, or visit in person — your NYC advantage)

What cuts through:
- A specific, verifiable observation ("your online ordering goes through DoorDash only — you're paying ~25% commission on every order")
- A compliance risk they didn't know they had (Section 4A/4B/4C)
- Timing: pre-open (nothing configured yet) or a known trigger (new liquor license, ownership change, lease filing)
- Referral framing: CPA/lawyer/accountant intros (95% close per Simon) — worth building a small CPA outreach track in parallel.

## 6. Fit with the existing plan

- The SLA pending-license bot = the "timing" angle (D + pre-open). Attach the compliance angles (4A–C) as the enrichment layer Simon said past lists lacked.
- Per Simon's own numbers, qualified cold leads close 15–20% and referrals 75% — so a 3–5% target may actually be underselling *if* leads are enriched and qualified. 3–5% is a realistic *response* rate for cold outreach, but closes on qualified handoffs should run higher.
- Enrichment fields to add per lead: platform detected, hosting, DoorDash/website presence, headcount estimate (payroll angle if 10+), one compliance flag, one-line pitch angle, contact.
- Batch by angle (all Toast users, all no-website pre-opens, all GoDaddy sites) so the pitch stays consistent — matches the one-platform-at-a-time plan.
